The first 48 hours

The part nobody explains while you're frightened.

What you do in the two days after an accident shapes everything that follows — whether or not you ever call us.

  1. Hour 0–2

    Get seen, and get it written down

    Medical attention first, always. A gap between the accident and the first medical record is the most common thing used to reduce a claim later.

  2. Hour 2–12

    Photograph everything

    Vehicles, the road, signage, your injuries, the weather. Scenes are cleared and cars are repaired far sooner than people expect.

  3. Hour 12–24

    Do not give a recorded statement

    The other driver's insurer will call quickly and sound helpful. You are not obliged to give a recorded account, and it is rarely wise unadvised.

  4. Hour 24–48

    Have someone read the release

    An early cheque usually comes attached to a release of all future claims — including injuries that have not shown up yet.

Our fee

You pay us out of the recovery.

Written plainly here, because the fee agreement is the thing clients least often read and most often regret.

  • Contingency, stated up front

    The percentage is in writing before you sign, and it does not move because the file got harder.

  • Costs itemised as we go

    Filing fees, records and experts — you see the running total, not one deduction at the end.

  • No recovery, no fee

    If the case resolves with nothing, you owe nothing — including costs we already advanced.

  • You decide whether to settle

    Every offer comes with our recommendation and our reasoning. The decision stays yours.
