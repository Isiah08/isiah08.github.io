The first 48 hours

The part nobody explains while you're frightened.

What you do in the two days after an accident shapes everything that follows. This holds true whether or not you ever call us.

  1. Hour 0–2At the scene

    Get seen, and get it written down

    Medical attention first, always. A gap between the accident and the first medical record is the single most common thing used to reduce a claim later.

  2. Hour 2–12Before anything is moved

    Photograph everything

    Vehicles, the road, signage, your injuries, the weather. Scenes are cleared and vehicles are repaired far sooner than people expect.

  3. Hour 12–24When the phone rings

    Do not give a recorded statement

    The other driver's insurer will call quickly and sound helpful. You are not obliged to give them a recorded account, and it is rarely in your interest to do so unadvised.

  4. Hour 24–48Before you sign

    Have someone read the release

    An early cheque usually comes attached to a release of all future claims — including for injuries that have not shown up yet. Have it read first.

Our fee

You pay us out of the recovery.

If there is no recovery there is no fee. Written plainly here because the fee agreement is the thing clients least often read and most often regret.

  • Contingency, stated up front

    The percentage is set out in writing before you sign, and it does not change because the file got harder.

  • Case costs explained separately

    Filing fees, records and experts are itemised. You see the running total, not a single deduction at the end.

  • No fee if we do not recover

    If the case resolves with nothing, you owe us nothing — including the costs we have already advanced.

  • You decide whether to settle

    Every offer comes to you with our recommendation and our reasoning. The decision stays yours.
